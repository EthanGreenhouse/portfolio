Asynchronous Buck Converter

February 2026Source on GitHub

  • KiCad
  • LTspice
  • Power Electronics
  • PCB Design

Overview

A 78 × 16 mm two-layer board taking 12 V in on a barrel jack to 5 V out on a USB-A socket at up to 3 A. My first switching regulator, sized from the datasheet rather than bought as a module.

A linear regulator doing the same job dissipates (12 - 5) × 3 = 21 W in the package, which is not coolable on a board this size and caps efficiency near 42%. The LM2596S-5 is a fixed 5 V step-down in TO-263 switching at 150 kHz. It is asynchronous, so a Schottky catch diode carries the freewheel current instead of a second FET: that costs efficiency but removes the gate driver and bootstrap supply.

Component selection

The target was continuous conduction at the full 3 A load, meaning inductor current never falls to zero during the switching cycle. Discontinuous operation is not dangerous, but it makes output voltage load dependent and worsens ripple.

PartValueWhy
L1 inductor33 µHFrom the LM2596 selection curves for 12 V in at 3 A. Sets ripple current, which decides whether conduction stays continuous.
C1 input680 µFA buck draws input current in pulses. Supplying them locally keeps them off the supply leads, which helps ripple and EMI.
C2 output220 µFAbsorbs inductor ripple current to flatten the rail, and sets transient response under a load step.
D1 catch diodeSS54 Schottky, 40 V, 5 ACarries inductor current during switch off. Schottky for low forward drop and fast recovery, rated above the 12 V input and 3 A load to cover transients.

Layout

Input, switch node, and output traces are widened to 0.8 mm to carry 3 A without meaningful drop or heating. The switch node matters most: it joins the regulator output pin, the inductor, and the catch diode while swinging the full input voltage at 150 kHz. Kept short it behaves like a trace, left to sprawl it behaves like an antenna. The long, narrow outline puts the barrel jack and USB-A socket at opposite ends, giving a straight through power path with the switching section in the middle.

Simulation

Modelled in LTspice as an open-loop, fixed-duty stage: an ideal high side switch, the SS54 as freewheel diode, and the parasitics that matter, 50 mΩ of inductor series resistance and 50 mΩ of output capacitor ESR. Figures are averaged over the last millisecond of a 5 ms transient, once the output has settled.

ResultValueDetail
Efficiency at 3 A91.3%16.64 W in, 15.19 W out
Catch diode loss0.90 W62% of the 1.45 W total, and the first thing a synchronous FET would recover
Inductor loss0.46 Wanother 32%, leaving about 6% in the switch and capacitor ESR combined
Inductor ripple0.63 Apeak to peak, minimum 2.71 A, so conduction stays continuous at full load
Output ripple31 mVon a 5.03 V rail

The loss breakdown is the useful part. Knowing the diode dominates tells you where a Rev B would go, and it is a better answer than assuming the regulator itself is the problem.

Status

Schematic and layout complete in KiCad, design simulated, board not fabricated, so every figure above is simulated rather than measured. Once built, the things worth putting on a scope are the switch node waveform, output ripple at full load, and efficiency across the load range against the 91.3% the model predicts.
