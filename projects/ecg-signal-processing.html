ECG Acquisition Circuit

November 2025 – December 2025Circuits and Signals Lab

  • Analog Design
  • MATLAB
  • Signal Processing

Overview

Millivolt signals from chest electrodes turned into a heart rate reading. Electrodes into an instrumentation amplifier, two filter stages, a 14-bit converter, and R-peak detection in MATLAB. Built over two lab cycles.

Signal chain

StagePartDesign value
Instrumentation ampAD627 10 kΩ gain resistor, dual 1.5 V AA supply
High-passLT1490 0.106 Hz, far below the ECG band, so it strips electrode DC offset without touching the signal
Low-pass, anti-aliasLT1490 159 Hz, above the 150 Hz of real content and below what would fold back when sampling
ConverterNI USB-6001 14-bit at 1 kHz, 1.22 mV per level
DetectionMATLAB findpeaks R-to-R interval averaged for BPM

The amplifier runs on batteries rather than a bench supply to keep it off AC mains, with 0.1 µF decoupling on both rails.

Measured

ParameterMeasuredReference
Differential gain21.525 theoretical
System gain, all three stages18
CMRR at 150 Hz70.5 dB80 to 85 dB datasheet
Heart rate, five clips65 to 95 BPM

I measured CMRR by feeding a 500 mV sine at 150 Hz into both inputs and reading 3.2 mV at the output. The shortfall against the datasheet is electrode impedance mismatch and breadboard layout. It was still enough to pull a clean QRS complex out of ambient noise.

Results

Five 10-second clips across rest, exercise, and deep breathing. R-peak detection ran cleanly on all five.

  • Resting: 72 BPM
  • Different subject, resting: 65 BPM
  • After running: 95 BPM
  • Deep breathing: 68 BPM
  • Slow movement: about 100 mV of baseline drift, most of it attenuated by the high-pass
