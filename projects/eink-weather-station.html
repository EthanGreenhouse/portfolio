Overview

A battery powered 2-layer ESP32-WROOM-32 board that pulls weather over WiFi onto a Waveshare 4.2 inch four-color e-paper panel. USB-C feeds a TP4056 charger, the cell feeds an AP2112K-3.3 regulator, and the ESP32 sleeps between hourly updates. Rev B.2 is 80 × 60 mm, 35 components, 52 nets, 49 vias, no DFM violations at JLCPCB's standard tier.

What Rev A got wrong

Rev A was fully routed and looked finished, but it would not have run on a battery. Two things had to change.

ChangeRev ARev B
Regulator AMS1117-3.3, 1.1 V dropout, 5 mA quiescent AP2112K-3.3 in SOT-23-5, 250 mV at 600 mA, 55 µA quiescent
ESP32 placement Mid-board with the antenna pointing inward Board edge, so the keep-out falls off the board entirely

A cell runs from 4.2 V down to 3.0 V, so after 1.1 V of dropout the 3.3 V rail would have sat near 2.9 V on a full charge, below the ESP32 minimum. The quiescent draw was a second problem on its own: 5 mA flattens a 2000 mAh cell in about two weeks doing nothing.

The Rev A keep-out carved a 48 × 21 mm strip of bare FR4 through the middle of the board, 37% of its area, and stranded the display header behind it. Six SPI signals and two stitching traces crossed the antenna region to reach it, detuning the antenna. I checked Rev B against the raw board geometry rather than trusting DRC: no copper between the board edge and the module's own pad row.

Rev B.2 layout. The hatched keep-out zone sits almost entirely off the board, which is the whole point of moving the module to the edge.

Power budget

Charge current500 mA2.4 kΩ program resistor, 0.25 C into the 2000 mAh pack
Awake26 s per houragainst a 100 µA sleep baseline
Daily draw13 mAh11 mAh active plus 2.4 mAh asleep
Runtimeabout 4 monthsper charge, calculated from datasheet figures rather than measured
Usable capacityabout 85%the AP2112K drops out near 3.55 V, well before the pack protection cuts off at 2.4 to 3.0 V
Battery sense1 M / 1 M into IO34an ADC1 channel, because ADC2 is unusable while WiFi is active

System load shares a node with the charger's battery pin, which can corrupt termination since the TP4056 stops at 50 mA. I planned a load sharing FET, then checked the duty cycle: 26 seconds an hour against a 100 µA baseline is roughly 500 times under the threshold, so it works as built. Bench development with USB connected is the exception. WiFi also has to be off before a panel refresh, since transmit peaks near 500 mA against the regulator's 600 mA rating.

Manufacturing

Inside JLCPCB's standard process at 0.25 mm minimum track, 0.416 mm spacing, and 0.3 mm drill. Two layers, all 27 SMD parts on the front for single sided assembly, two through hole connectors, fiducials present, zero violations.

The accepted tradeoff is grounding. 25 bottom layer segments cut the ground pour, worst case 28.6% reference coverage on the display chip select net. That is a radiated emissions concern rather than a functional one, and SPI to a slow panel will work fine, but it would need fixing on a board seeking certification.

Status

Design frozen in August 2026 and cleared for fabrication, with gerbers, BOM, and pick and place files generated. The board has not been built yet. Bring-up order:

  • Meter the battery connector polarity first. JST PH pigtails are not consistently wired and there is no reverse polarity protection on this board.
  • Power from USB only and confirm 3.3 V at the display header before connecting the panel.
  • Measure sleep current against the 100 µA estimate.
  • Isolate how much the display module's level translator draws, since it is permanently powered with no way to shut it off.
