Overview

Six months as a Quality Engineer at AvtechTyee, an aerospace manufacturer, on two projects: replacing manual incoming inspection of circuit board assemblies with machine vision, and building a model that predicts which suppliers are most likely to cause quality problems.

Machine vision inspection

Incoming inspection was manual. An inspector compared each board against documentation by eye, which is slow and depends on who is working that day, and a board that passes while carrying a defect is far more expensive to find later in the build.

I set up a Keyence VS-series system to do the comparison automatically. The camera mounts on an adjustable arm over a fixed stage so board position and lighting stay consistent between units, and each board type gets a program that captures reference images and compares incoming units against them. Differences show up as a heat map, so for a missing component, a backwards component, or foreign material the operator sees the location rather than just a pass or fail.

Supplier risk model

Predicting supplier quality problems before they turn into nonconformance reports, using seven years of history at supplier-month granularity.

The hard part was defining the target. "This supplier is getting worse" is easy to say and difficult to write down. I settled on a trend label: a supplier is positive for a given month if their nonconformance rate over the next three months is at least 1.5 times their rate over the previous three. That alone produced a lot of noise, since a supplier with zero reports in the past three months and one in the next is mathematically a large increase and operationally nothing. Requiring a minimum count in the forward window removed most of those. 

# "Trend up" means future_avg >= multiple * past_avg.

ClassifierHistogram gradient boosting, with logistic regression kept alongside as a baseline through the same preprocessing
CalibrationIsotonic. With roughly ten positives per fold, sigmoid compressed nearly every prediction toward the base rate; isotonic gave a better Brier score and a wider spread
ValidationWalk-forward backtest. A random split would let the model train on later months and score earlier ones

Predictions are calibrated so the output reads as a probability rather than only a ranking, which matters because the threshold is a business decision about how many suppliers the team can review.
